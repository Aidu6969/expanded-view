## 2. Common myths / things to avoid stating

| Myth often repeated online | What the sources actually support |
|---|---|
| "Ayrton Senna developed/designed the NSX" | Senna drove a prototype (Feb 1989) and called it "a little fragile". Honda then raised its rigidity targets (Apr 1989). The 50% rigidity gain came from the Nürburgring programme, and the development driver was Motoharu Kurosawa. Say he **gave feedback that influenced** the stiffening, not that he developed the car. ([HONDA-EP], [WIKI-1G]) |
| "Senna made Honda stiffen the chassis by 50%" | Honda ties the +50% figure to the German testing. Don't link the 50% to Senna. |
| "The NSX was designed by Pininfarina" | Only the 1984 HP-X concept was Pininfarina's. Wikipedia credits the production car's design to Honda's in-house team (chief designer Masahito Nakano). |
| "The first aluminium car ever" | Honda's own wording is "all-aluminum monocoque body — a world's first for a **production** vehicle at the time." Keep that qualifier, since aluminium bodywork existed on earlier low-volume cars. |
| "The McLaren F1 was based on / copied the NSX" | Murray said the NSX's **ride quality and handling** became the F1's design target. He also asked Honda for an engine, was turned down, and used a BMW V12. The quote comes from a translated Japanese magazine article cited on Wikipedia. Attribute it as "Murray later wrote..." and don't overstate it. |
| "Senna personally set the redline / chose VTEC" | No reliable source says this. |
| "Hand-built, like a Ferrari, every panel by hand" | Honda says the plant had no conveyor and relied on highly skilled manual finishing, at about 25 cars a day. Say "largely hand-assembled at a dedicated low-volume plant". Don't say "every car was hand-built by one craftsman". |
| "8,300 rpm redline" | Honda's own engine page says the red zone starts at 8,000 rpm (manual). |
| "NSX means 'New Sports eXperimental'" / "Nouveau Sport X" etc. | Acura's release gives "New Sportscar eXperimental". |
| "F-16 canopy" as a Honda claim | This appears on Wikipedia, and no Honda page was found that confirms it. Phrase it as "Uehara reportedly studied an F-16 cockpit's all-round visibility". |
| "Exactly 18,000 built" | Honda says "more than 18,000" were sold as of June 2005. |
| "The NSX forced Ferrari to build the F355" | No reliable source states a direct causal link. Safer: the NSX showed that a supercar could be usable every day (Hagerty), and rivals were measured against it. |

## 4. Suggested 8-chapter outline (target 1,500–1,700 words of narration)

**Open loop (hook):** "Ayrton Senna made the NSX a supercar." That's the legend. Senna did drive a prototype and told Honda one blunt thing about it. But what actually made the car stiffer happened on a different track, and the truth is more interesting. Pay this off in chapter 5 or 6: the "a little fragile" quote, rigidity targets raised in April 1989, and the +50% from the Nürburgring. Second loop: "and one of the greatest car designers alive threw out his list of benchmark cars after driving it" (Murray, paid off in chapter 7).

1. **Cold open (~120 words):** The Senna myth set against the fact that Honda's own records tell a different story. One line on the stakes: a Japanese company taking on Ferrari in 1990.
2. **The idea (~200 words):** The 1984 UMR test mule built from a Honda City. The Pininfarina HP-X concept. Full development from 1985, led by Shigeru Uehara. The name, "New Sportscar eXperimental". (Facts 1–6)
3. **The aluminium gamble (~220 words):** The world's first all-aluminium monocoque for a production car (Honda's own claim, with the qualifier). Nearly 200 kg saved, five alloys, the 600 °C extrusion, the new spot welder, and a dedicated plant with no conveyor belt. (Facts 11–14, 32–33)
4. **The heart (~220 words):** C30A, VTEC added late, titanium rods (190 g lighter, +700 rpm), 8,000 rpm red zone, 280 PS in Japan and 270 hp in the US. The F-16 canopy, phrased as "reportedly". (Facts 15–23)
5. **The Green Hell (~200 words):** The Nürburgring base in Müllenbach, the first long-term overseas test by a Japanese carmaker, and the +50% rigidity gain. A month at Suzuka. (Facts 25–27)
6. **Enter Senna (~180 words):** Pay off the hook. February 1989, the "a little fragile" quote, rigidity targets raised in April. Make clear Kurosawa was the development driver and Senna gave feedback rather than developing the car. (Facts 28–29)
7. **Launch and shock (~220 words):** Chicago, 9 Feb 1989. Sales from September 1990 in Japan and August 1990 in the US at $65,000. Three-year waits. Motor Trend's praise. Gordon Murray's verdict and the F1 engine Honda refused to build. A usable exotic set against the Ferrari 348. (Facts 7–10, 30–31, 34, 42, 46)
8. **Evolution, end and rebirth (~240 words):** NSX-R 1992 (483 cars), NSX-T 1995, 3.2 L and 6-speed in 1997, the 2002 facelift that dropped the pop-up headlights, NSX-R NA2. Production ends in late 2005 after just over 18,000 cars.
