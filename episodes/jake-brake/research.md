# Jake brake (compression release engine brake): research

| # | Claim in the video | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The machine-gun / jackhammer sound comes from trucks using compression brakes, especially without mufflers | Wikipedia: "may cause a vehicle to make a loud 'growling', 'machine gun', or 'jackhammer' like exhaust noise, especially vehicles having no mufflers" https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compression_release_engine_brake |
| 2 | Name "Jake brake": Jacobs was the first company to make them | Wikipedia: "The first company to manufacture them was Jacobs Vehicle Systems, resulting in the term Jake brake." |
| 3 | Long steep grades; keeps the service (wheel) brakes cool for emergencies | ETHW (ASME landmark): "permits large trucks to descend long, steep grades at a controlled speed ... allowing service brakes to remain cool for emergency situations" https://ethw.org/ASME-Landmark:Jacobs_Engine_Brake_Retarder |
| 4 | Without the brake, compressed air pushes the piston back down and returns its energy | Wikipedia: "the compressed air mixture returns its energy to the piston by pushing the piston back down" |
| 5 | The brake opens the exhaust valve just before the end of the compression stroke; the energy isn't returned | Wikipedia: "opens exhaust valves to the cylinders, right before the compression stroke ends ... the air compression energy is not returned to the crankshaft"; ETHW: "no pressure remains in the cylinder to act on the piston" |
| 6 | Uses engine oil pressure (hydraulic) | Wikipedia: "a hydraulic system using engine oil which transfers the motion of the fuel injector rocker arm to the engine's exhaust valve(s)" (the animation shows a master piston on the injector rocker and a slave piston on the exhaust valve) |
| 7 | Off the fuel / used when the driver lifts off | Wikipedia: activation "occurs when both the clutch and the throttle are released with the transmission in gear" |
| 8 | Turns the engine into an air compressor | ETHW: "by turning a diesel engine into an air compressor" |
| 9 | Many towns post "No Engine Brake" signs | Wikipedia: many communities in the US, Canada and Australia prohibit compression braking; signs such as "No Engine Brake", "Unmuffled Engine Braking Prohibited"; SlashGear https://www.slashgear.com/1943176/what-do-no-engine-brake-signs-mean/ |
| 10 | Six cylinders, firing order 1-5-3-6-2-4 (on screen) | Typical heavy-truck diesels are inline sixes; 1-5-3-6-2-4 is the standard inline-six order (same as our 2JZ episode). The video says "six cylinders" as an example, not a claim about every truck. |

Unsure / resolved:
- SlashGear describes Jake brakes as creating exhaust "back pressure" (that is an *exhaust brake*, a different device). Not used; Wikipedia + ETHW agree on the compression-release mechanism.
- No weight or temperature numbers stated (80,000 lb not found in the sources read). The pressure graph has no units (illustrative, compression ratio 16 assumed for shape only).
- Invention: ETHW says invented 1957 by Clessie Cummins, produced by Jacobs since 1961; patent 1965. Only in the description ("Clessie Cummins invented the system").
