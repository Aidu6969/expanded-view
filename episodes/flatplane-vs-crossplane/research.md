# Flat plane vs cross plane V8 — research (2026-11-11)

Every fact the narration states, with a source.

1. **Cross-plane V8: four crankpins 90 degrees apart, forming a cross seen from the end.** Wikipedia: the four
   crankpins are "offset at 90° from the adjacent crankpins", "in two planes crossed at 90°". Autocar: conventional
   V8 journals "at 90deg to one another". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossplane · https://www.autocar.co.uk/node/105081
2. **Muscle cars / American trucks use cross-plane.** Jalopnik: "In most versions of the engine – such as those found
   in American trucks and muscle cars" the layout gives the throaty warble; "most V8 trucks are cross-plane".
   Wikipedia: cross-plane is the most popular configuration in V8 road cars.
   https://jalopnik.com/1878133/why-flat-plane-crank-v8-engines-sound-different/
3. **A V8 fires every 90 degrees overall (even), but each bank fires unevenly.** Wikipedia gives the per-bank intervals
   as "180-90-180-270 crankshaft degrees" (same cycle as the 90/180/270/180 we draw: short, long, longer, long).
   Autocar: "one cylinder can fire after another in the same bank". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossplane
4. **Each bank has its own exhaust; the uneven per-bank pulses are the burble.** Wikipedia: each bank's four exhaust
   ports merge into one exit, and the uneven pattern produces the "potato-potato" burble; equal-length 180-degree
   ("bundle of snakes", Ford GT40) headers that cross banks are needed to even it out. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossplane
5. **Flat-plane: all four pins in one plane, 180 degrees (half a turn) apart; firing alternates left, right, left,
   right.** Autocar: journals "at 180deg to one another"; firing "moves sequentially from one cylinder bank to the other:
   left, right, left, right and so on". Jalopnik: "Left, right, left, right, forever." https://www.autocar.co.uk/node/105081
6. **Each bank then fires evenly, like two four-cylinders.** Autocar: it "fires like two screaming four-pots together",
   exhaust pulses "follow one after the other". (Each bank of four fires every 180 degrees, exactly like an inline four.)
7. **Lighter crank, revs higher.** Autocar: "Flat-plane cranks are lighter", which "lets it rev higher". Wikipedia:
   cross-plane V8s have heavy counterweights and "very heavy crankshafts, meaning they are not as free revving".
   Jalopnik: lighter cranks rev higher, "which makes for a higher pitch".
8. **The catch: it shakes like a four-cylinder; that's why big V8s stayed cross-plane.** Autocar: "inferior balance
   (piston pairs move in the same direction, like in a four-pot engine)". Wikipedia: cross-plane was developed because
   the flat-plane's second-order forces "stack up and become noticeable in large displacement engines".
9. **Ferrari V8s are flat-plane.** Ferrari F154 (current Ferrari twin-turbo V8 family) uses a flat-plane crankshaft.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrari_F154_engine · Jalopnik associates the sound with Ferrari.
10. **The (2023) Corvette Z06 went flat-plane, to 8,600 rpm.** Motor Authority: LT6 has "a flat-plane crankshaft", tach
    can "fly to a lofty 8,600 rpm". https://www.motorauthority.com/news/1132938_2023-chevrolet-corvette-z06-debuts-october-26-with-screaming-flat-plane-crank-v-8-and-8-600-rpm-redline

## Firing model used in the animation (720-degree cycle)
- Cross-plane (GM order 1-8-4-3-6-5-7-2, left bank odd): left bank fires at 0, 270, 450, 540; right at 90, 180, 360, 630.
  Per-bank gaps: left 270/180/90/180, right 90/180/270/180 (matches Wikipedia's 180-90-180-270 cycle). Union: every 90.
- Flat-plane: left 0, 180, 360, 540; right 90, 270, 450, 630. Per bank every 180; union every 90.

## Misconceptions avoided
- The cross-plane V8 is NOT uneven-firing overall; it fires every 90 degrees. Only each bank is uneven.
- Pitch also rises with rpm (Jalopnik); we say both: even per-bank pulses AND the lighter crank revving higher.
- "Muscle car" is a generalisation (most are cross-plane); the Mustang GT350 Voodoo flat-plane is not claimed.
- We do not claim the old Z06s were flat-plane; "then the Corvette Z06 went flat plane" refers to the 2023 LT6.
- Animation slowed; all engine sounds synthesized from the firing model (no recordings of real cars).
