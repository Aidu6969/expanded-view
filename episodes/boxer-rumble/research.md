# Boxer rumble: research

Sources read 2026-11-08. Every claim the narration makes is below.

## Facts stated in the video
1. **The rumble is not simply the boxer layout.** "The rumble comes from the exhaust layout, not simply from the boxer engine." "The boxer layout contributes to the character, but runner length and collector geometry are the reason one Subaru flat-four can rumble while another sounds comparatively even." https://thinktuning.com/wrx-unequal-length-headers/ . Motor Authority: the boxer design is "only part of what gives it that unique sound"; "it's more about the design of the headers." https://www.motorauthority.com/news/1104225_that-subaru-rumble-heres-why-that-happens
2. **A flat-four fires evenly, one cylinder every 180 crank degrees (half a turn).** Firing interval "evenly spaced at 180 degrees." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat-four_engine . SlashGear: a boxer has "an even firing sequence." https://www.slashgear.com/1970710/subaru-unequal-length-headers-rumble-sound
3. **Pistons work in opposing pairs.** Horizontally opposed layout (Wikipedia, above).
4. **With even pipes it should sound even.** SlashGear: with evenly spaced headers "it'd have an even-sounding exhaust note." Jalopnik: equal-length waveform would look "quite even." https://jalopnik.com/1933843/subaru-unequal-header-length-exhaust-sound/
5. **One bank's pipes run under the engine; the other is inches from the turbo inlet.** One bank's pipes "run underneath the engine" and join the other bank's pipe "which is mere inches from the turbocharger exhaust inlet." (Jalopnik.)
6. **Pipes from the two banks differ in length.** "The header piping from cylinders one and three are of a different length than those from two and four." (Motor Authority.)
7. **Pulses leave evenly but reach the collector unevenly.** ThinkTuning: "The engine's firing order has not changed. What changes is when those pulses arrive together"; unequal runners send pulses to the collector "in an off-beat pattern." SlashGear: "the sound exits in lumps" because one side's exhaust "has a longer distance to cover." Not Just Car Noises: unequal spacing creates half-orders, which give the rumble. https://carnoise.substack.com/p/dat-sweet-rumble-tho
8. **The 2015 WRX moved to equal-length headers to feed a twin-scroll turbo.** ThinkTuning table: 2015-2021 WRX FA20DIT equal length, twin-scroll; "Subaru gave the FA20DIT and FA24DIT equal-length headers to feed those twin-scroll turbine inlets cleanly." Motor Authority (2016): the 2.0-litre WRX "has equal length headers, and thus it lacks the rumble" of the 2.5 STI.
9. **The WRX STI kept unequal-length headers through 2021.** ThinkTuning table: 2004-2021 WRX STI, EJ257, unequal; "The VA STI retained the EJ layout even while the VA WRX moved to the FA20DIT."
10. **Unequal-length headers are sold for the newer cars.** Tomei Expreme unequal-length manifold for 2015-2021 WRX: https://www.maperformance.com/products/tomei-expreme-series-unequal-length-headers-2015-2020-subaru-wrx-tb6010-sb04a . ThinkTuning's guide covers an unequal-length header kit for FA cars.

## What the animation shows (not narrated as numbers)
- Sequence of firing is bank then bank, per Wikipedia ("the left bank fires one after another, then the right bank"). Crank phasing follows this: each opposing pair moves together, the two pairs are 180 degrees apart.
- Pulse delay comes from the on-screen pipe length at a constant speed. **The time shift is deliberately exaggerated** so viewers can see it, and the animation is far slower than a real engine. Both are said in the description and a slow-motion note is on screen. Real length differences are not given in any source, so no number is stated.
- Generic car, no badges. All sound effects, including the rumble, are synthesized here (ev/sfx is paused with ElevenLabs).

## Unsure, and how it was resolved
- **Does equal length remove the rumble completely?** Sources disagree: SlashGear "eliminate the burble altogether"; Motor Authority "lacks the rumble"; Wikipedia's 360-degree pairing gives "a less distinctive exhaust sound"; Not Just Car Noises finds equal-length Subarus still "rumbly enough", with a less pronounced beating. The narration therefore says "most of the rumble went away", and the description says some remains.
- **Why did Subaru use unequal pipes?** Subaru has not said definitively (SlashGear). Jalopnik and others suggest packaging. The video does not claim a reason.
- **Why did Subaru switch?** ThinkTuning gives twin-scroll feeding. Subaru's own statement was not found. The narration uses ThinkTuning's reason, which is the only sourced one.
- **Firing order number** (commonly listed as 1-3-2-4) is not stated in the video; the bank-then-bank sequence is the sourced part.

## Misconceptions avoided
- "Boxer engines rumble because they are boxers": the layout alone does not do it.
- "It's misfiring / uneven firing": the firing stays even, only arrival is uneven.
